Its original result remains failed and incomplete at 17 configurations and 51 profiles. The missing checking-leg outcome and original wrapper process receipt remain absent. The separate finalization validates both complete product receipts against the frozen 7,500-input source cohort: configuration bindings, binary and dump content, every resource tree, retained link objects and archive, and product metadata. Both application signatures pass strict deep verification and no open handles remain. A real read-only verification subprocess finishes successfully before any retirement intent is written. This is fresh file verification; the original compiler's final exit status remains unknown. No compiler process receipt is fabricated or inferred from preservation cleanup.

Only two consumed objects/units duplicate trees are retired through the canonical retirement API: 10,036 files and 344 directories, containing 335,691,785 logical bytes and 355,475,456 bytes of prior per-path allocation. All 9,520 retained product witnesses remain identical. The ledger exposes opaque path identifiers, content hashes, sizes and modes; private generated filenames and host identities remain private. Prior per-path allocation is not claimed as exclusive physical recovery or a volume-free-space delta. Regeneration from retained frozen inputs is the recovery boundary; original generated names and compiler timing are not promised. Permanent objects, link archives, programs, dumps, resources, product receipts and original failed execution evidence are retained.

It preserves the original seventeen completed configurations, then separately adopts the verified configuration and four profiles to establish an 18/20 configuration, 55/61 profile checkpoint. Its pure-file plan passes before the remaining two configurations are built as a separate controlled workload. Adoption records a finalization proof and a verification subprocess, without representing either as the missing historical compiler process.
